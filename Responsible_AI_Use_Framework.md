# Executive Summary

Artificial intelligence is becoming an integral part of modern work. It can improve productivity, strengthen analysis, accelerate learning, and support better decision-making. However, AI does not replace human responsibility.

Responsible AI use is not defined by the technology itself, but by how people choose to use it.

This framework provides practical principles for the responsible use of AI in professional environments. It is intended as a reference that organizations can adapt to their own governance models, policies, regulatory obligations, and risk environments.

The central principle of this framework is simple:

> **AI is a tool. Responsibility remains with the person who uses it.**

---

# Purpose

The purpose of this framework is to provide practical guidance for the responsible use of artificial intelligence in professional work.

It focuses on individual responsibility, professional judgment, confidentiality, fairness, and preventing harm while encouraging organizations to use AI productively and responsibly.

---

# Foundations

This framework is informed by the Responsible AI principles developed by Professor Luciano Floridi and colleagues:

- Explicability
- Autonomy
- Beneficence
- Justice
- Non-maleficence

These principles are interpreted through the practical realities of enterprise AI use, where responsibility always remains with human users.

Accordingly, this framework organizes responsible AI use into five practical principles:

1. Accountability and Explainability
2. Human Judgment and Control
3. Legitimate and Beneficial Use
4. Fairness and Respect for People
5. Do No Harm

---

# Principle 1 – Accountability and Explainability

People remain accountable for work assisted by AI.

AI may draft, summarize, analyze, translate, or recommend content, but responsibility for the final work always belongs to the individual who chooses to use it.

Good practice includes:

- Reviewing AI-generated outputs before use.
- Understanding how AI contributed to the work.
- Being able to explain important AI-assisted decisions.
- Accepting responsibility for the quality and consequences of AI-assisted work.

**Key message**

> You own what you use.

---

# Principle 2 – Human Judgment and Control

AI should support professional judgment, not replace it.

AI systems may produce outputs that appear confident while being inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, biased, or fabricated. Human review remains essential.

Good practice includes:

- Applying professional judgment.
- Validating important outputs.
- Using AI as decision support rather than decision authority.
- Maintaining appropriate human oversight for material decisions.

**Key message**

> AI can assist, but it cannot decide for you.

---

# Principle 3 – Legitimate and Beneficial Use

AI should be used for legitimate purposes that improve the quality, effectiveness, and integrity of work.

Responsible AI use is not simply about efficiency. It is about achieving better outcomes while maintaining professional standards.

Good practice includes:

- Using AI for lawful and ethical purposes.
- Improving quality rather than avoiding responsibility.
- Supporting learning, analysis, communication, and decision preparation.
- Avoiding deception, manipulation, impersonation, or misuse.

**Key message**

> Use AI to strengthen good work, not to create shortcuts around responsibility.

---

# Principle 4 – Fairness and Respect for People

AI should not create or reinforce unfair outcomes.

Where AI-assisted work affects people, additional care should be taken to ensure fairness, transparency, and appropriate human review.

Organizations should pay particular attention when AI influences decisions involving employees, customers, applicants, patients, suppliers, or other stakeholders.

**Key message**

> AI efficiency is not responsible if it produces unfair outcomes.

---

# Principle 5 – Do No Harm

AI should be used in ways that minimize foreseeable harm.

Potential harms include:

- Confidentiality breaches
- Data leakage
- Poor decision-making
- Security risks
- Regulatory violations
- Reputational damage
- Unfair treatment

Responsible users understand that using AI does not reduce existing obligations relating to privacy, confidentiality, intellectual property, cybersecurity, or professional conduct.

**Key message**

> If you cannot tell an unauthorized person, do not tell an unauthorized AI tool.

---

# Practical Questions Before Using AI

Before relying on AI, consider:

1. Can I safely share this information with this AI tool?
2. Am I prepared to stand behind the output?
3. Have I reviewed the output for accuracy and context?
4. Am I acting within my authority?
5. Could this negatively affect another person?
6. Could this create avoidable harm?

If the answer to any question is uncertain, seek appropriate guidance before proceeding.

# Illustrative Examples

### Appropriate Use

- Drafting non-confidential documents.
- Improving writing quality.
- Brainstorming ideas.
- Summarizing approved information.
- Supporting research and analysis.
- Preparing first drafts subject to human review.

### Inappropriate Use

- Uploading confidential information to unauthorized AI systems.
- Accepting AI output without review.
- Making important decisions solely using AI.
- Misrepresenting AI-generated work as verified.
- Using AI to bypass governance or approval processes.
- Blaming AI for professional mistakes.

---

# Applying This Framework

Every organization has different governance requirements, regulatory obligations, and risk tolerances.

This framework is intended to serve as a practical reference that organizations may adapt when developing their own AI governance policies, standards, training, and operating procedures.

---

# Closing Statement

Responsible AI begins with responsible people.

AI can assist, accelerate, and augment professional work, but it does not replace accountability, judgment, confidentiality, fairness, or professional responsibility.

> **AI is a tool. Responsibility remains with the person who uses it.**
